# Part A — Product suggestions

## 1. Match-to-conversation starter

**Proposal:** When two people mutually add/like each other, show a lightweight conversation starter experience: a few optional prompts based on shared interests, plus a clear next step to start a conversation.

**Why it is required:** A mutual match is a moment of high intent, but users may not know what to say next. A small set of relevant, optional prompts can reduce the awkwardness of initiating a conversation and help convert a match into a meaningful interaction. It also gives the match screen a useful action rather than making it only a confirmation screen.

**Privacy consideration:** Prompts should be based only on information both matched users have chosen to make visible. The feature must not reveal one-sided likes or expose who added whom first.

## 2. Crush-list controls and privacy dashboard

**Proposal:** Add a simple privacy/control area where users can review and remove their own submitted crushes, correct an entry, and block/report an account where appropriate. Explain in plain language what is visible and when a match is revealed.

**Why it is required:** Adding a crush can feel sensitive. Users need confidence that they can correct mistakes and control their own submissions. Clear explanations reduce uncertainty, while block/report controls support user safety. This reinforces Coy's central promise that a person's interest is not disclosed unless it is mutual.

**Privacy consideration:** The dashboard must show only the user's own submitted entries and their own actions. It must never reveal incoming one-sided likes, identities, or counts that could be used to infer who liked the user.
